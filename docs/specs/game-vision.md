# Game Vision: Working Design Direction

> **Status:** Product planning document, not an implementation spec.
>
> **Working title:** Rags to Riches (provisional; likely to change)
>
> This document captures the game direction discussed by Paige and ChatGPT on 2026-09-27. It is intended to preserve product intent across ChatGPT, Claude Code, and future sessions without forcing unresolved ideas into implementation requirements.

## Core Concept

The game is inspired by the social ecosystem of ensemble reality-TV shows about wealthy women, especially the dynamics around status, cast hierarchy, money, marriages, gossip, alliances, public image, and producer-driven drama.

The player begins as an ordinary person and gradually works their way into this world.

The high-level progression is:

1. **Ordinary-life / social-climbing sim**
2. **Reality-show career**
3. **Escalating scandals and social mysteries**
4. **Cast trip**
5. **Murder mystery**

The murder should feel like an escalation of systems the player already understands, not a sudden genre switch.

The goal is to build an original game inspired by reality-TV dynamics rather than recreate specific shows, casts, incidents, or storylines.

## Core Fantasy

The player starts outside a glamorous social ecosystem and wants access.

At first, success means things like:

- earning enough money to improve their lifestyle
- buying better clothes and accessories
- getting invited to better events
- meeting wealthier or more connected people
- building relationships
- increasing social standing
- becoming someone people want at their parties

Eventually the aspiration changes from simply being wealthy or adjacent to wealth to becoming important enough that the cameras follow you.

The player can then experience the cost of maintaining that status.

## Design Principle: One Game That Deepens Over Time

The game should not feel like three disconnected games.

The preferred structure is:

**simple life sim -> social climb -> reality-TV career -> murder mystery**

Mechanics introduced early should deepen or change meaning later.

Examples:

- **Relationships** begin as friends, coworkers, romantic partners, and acquaintances. Later they become cast alliances, rivalries, producer relationships, witnesses, suspects, and sources.
- **Money** begins as rent, food, work, and basic lifestyle choices. Later it becomes designer goods, houses, businesses, cast salary, sponsorships, legal bills, and maintaining appearances.
- **Gossip** begins as small social information. Later it becomes anonymous leaks, tabloids, cast warfare, and eventually evidence.
- **Events** begin as birthdays, dinners, work events, and parties. Later they become launch parties, filmed dinners, cast trips, reunions, and major crime-scene settings.
- **Privacy choices** begin with ordinary relationship boundaries. Later they become decisions about what to expose on camera, what to leak, and what to hide during an investigation.
- **Social investigation** begins with small inconsistencies and rumors. Later it becomes full murder investigation.

A useful rule:

> Every major mechanic introduced early should either deepen or change meaning later.

## Early Game: Straightforward Social-Climbing Sim

The early game can be relatively simple and accessible.

A useful structural reference is the progression fantasy of older celebrity/social-climbing games such as *Kim Kardashian: Hollywood*, but without copying its content and without relying on repetitive mobile-style energy grinding.

The basic early loop should be:

**find work -> earn and save -> stabilize housing -> maintain friendships/dating -> take selective social opportunities -> gain access -> unlock better work and social opportunities**

The current starting direction is intentionally lower-status:

- unemployed
- staying with an established friend rather than owning or renting a place
- thin savings
- normal friends
- ordinary dating-app options, including plenty of duds
- little or no fame
- limited social access
- a distant ambition of eventually reaching the fictional reality-show world

The player's immediate problems should dominate the opening. The show can exist as background aspiration, but early job, housing, money, friendship, dating, and social decisions must be worthwhile on their own.

Early choices should already express the central tension.

Example:

The player has limited money and time. They can:

- work and earn money
- buy an expensive outfit for an important event
- spend time with their spouse
- attend a networking opportunity
- stay home and recover

The game should ask, even early:

> What are you willing to trade for a better life, and when can you afford to chase the bigger dream?

Early progress should involve real opportunity costs. Time spent job hunting cannot also be spent networking or dating. Money spent on clothes, dates, or events is money not saved toward independent housing. Social progress may be possible before the player is stable, but it should be harder to sustain.


## Career as a Persistent Progression Track

Career should remain an active system across the whole game, not disappear after the opening tutorial.

A useful progression shape is:

**unemployed -> survival job -> better job -> higher-status or better-connected work -> career/business that can continue into the show era**

Jobs should not be simple linear upgrades. Each role can trade off:

- income
- schedule flexibility
- stability
- status
- social access
- exposure to connected people
- future advancement

A higher-paying job may help the player move out faster but leave less time for social opportunities. A hospitality, events, luxury, media, or client-facing role may create more access while paying less or being less stable.

The player may keep a good job after joining the show. That should create competing priorities rather than becoming obsolete.

Possible show-era career pressure includes:

- filming conflicts with work
- missed shifts or client obligations
- unpaid leave
- employers enjoying publicity until the player creates reputational risk
- coworkers or clients reacting to aired behavior
- fans or critics appearing at a workplace
- pressure not to discuss an employer or business on camera
- promotions or major career opportunities competing with filming
- choosing whether unstable show income is enough to leave a reliable career

If the player owns or runs a business later, television exposure can help or hurt it. A strong public image may bring customers and sponsorships. A scandal may damage the business, drive away clients, strain employees, or turn the business itself into a storyline.

Core rule:

> Career evolves from survival -> stability -> status/access -> something fame can threaten.

## Wealth and Status

Money remains important throughout the game.

However, wealth should not be represented only as a cash balance.

Useful concepts include:

### Cash
What the player can actually spend.

### Income
Possible sources:

- ordinary job
- business
- wealthy spouse or partner
- show salary
- sponsorships
- appearances
- other opportunities

### Lifestyle
Visible consumption and assets such as:

- home
- wardrobe
- designer handbags
- jewelry
- cars
- glam
- vacations
- parties
- staff or assistants later in the game

### Debt / Financial Risk
The gap between lifestyle and actual financial stability.

This can create future storylines involving:

- overspending
- hidden debt
- questionable business practices
- legal bills
- tax problems
- shady investors
- dependence on a spouse

### Perceived Wealth
How rich other people think the player is.

Perceived wealth may differ substantially from reality.

A player could be:

- genuinely rich and visibly rich
- wealthy but understated
- cash-poor while maintaining an expensive image
- newly wealthy and insecure about it
- dependent on a spouse
- pretending to have more money than they do

Financial inconsistencies can become gossip, leverage, scandal, or motive later.

## Access as Social Currency

Status should be broader than wealth.

Potential dimensions include:

- money
- visible lifestyle
- taste / presentation
- access
- connections
- hosting power
- perceived wealth
- relevance
- public image

The player may become valuable to other people because they:

- host parties everyone wants to attend
- know important people
- have access to exclusive venues
- bring attention
- have social connections
- help other people gain status
- create entertaining storylines

This creates an important asymmetry:

> People may dislike the player while still wanting access to them.

Likability and usefulness should not be the same thing.

Someone could have:

- low affection
- low trust
- high envy
- high dependence
- high social value

and still remain socially close to the player.

A useful theme:

> Privilege buys tolerance, but not loyalty.

If the player loses the money, access, relevance, or connections that made people tolerate them, old grudges may suddenly matter.

## Parties and Hosting

Hosting should be a major expression of rising status.

Possible progression:

- apartment drinks
- birthday dinner
- small networking event
- launch party
- charity event
- elaborate anniversary
- invitation-only dinner
- cast event
- season-finale party
- cast trip hosting or planning

Guest lists should matter.

Choices may include:

- inviting a rival because it will create drama
- excluding someone
- inviting someone production wants there
- choosing between social safety and television value
- using an invitation as leverage

Eventually the player can move from desperately trying to get invited to hosting events other people are desperate to attend.

## Entry Into the Reality-Show World

The player should not immediately become a full cast member.

A useful ladder is:

**Outsider -> socially connected -> Guest -> Friend Of -> Full-Time Cast Member -> Veteran / Center Cast**

These are not necessarily permanent promotions.

The player can be demoted, paused, fired, or brought back.

### Guest
The player appears in isolated scenes or events but is not a formal cast member.

### Friend Of
The player appears regularly and is connected to the group but is not part of the main cast.

### Full-Time Cast Member
The player receives full cast status, opening-credit placement, stronger story expectations, and more production pressure.

### Veteran / Center Cast
The player becomes central to the show and its storylines.

This should create more prestige but also more pressure.

## Demotion, Firing, and Comebacks

Removal from the show should not automatically be game over.

Possible states:

- demoted from full-time to Friend Of
- temporarily "on pause"
- fired
- former cast member with off-camera influence
- return / comeback

While off the show, the player can still:

- affect cast relationships
- feed gossip
- leak information
- influence storylines
- repair family relationships
- focus on business
- manage money
- maintain public relevance
- cultivate current cast connections
- try to trigger a return

A useful distinction:

### Cast Status
The player's official contractual position on the show.

### Relevance
How much the season revolves around the player, whether or not they are officially cast.

A former cast member could be fired but still dominate the season because everyone keeps talking about them.

### Comeback Paths

Possible comeback routes include:

- audience interest
- unresolved feud
- relationship with a current cast member
- business or public success
- scandal
- being invited back for one scene that grows into a larger role
- creating enough drama that production cannot ignore the player

A darker route can involve sabotaging a vulnerable current cast member so that they are demoted or fired, creating an opening for the player to return.

This should not be a simple button. It should emerge through social choices such as:

- leaking damaging information
- encouraging confrontations
- exposing contradictions
- feeding production information
- turning allies against someone

Possible comeback styles:

- organic comeback
- storyline comeback
- strategic comeback
- sabotage comeback

Consequences should follow the player if people later discover they engineered another cast member's downfall.

## Production as a System

The show itself should feel like a machine with its own incentives.

Possible production interactions:

- producers suggesting scenes
- producers pushing certain confrontations
- confessionals
- filming schedules
- requests to discuss private topics
- editing choices
- contract decisions
- requests to invite certain people to events

A core tension:

> The player's private life is both something they want to protect and the exact thing the show wants to turn into content.

The game can explore the difference between:

- what happened
- what people claim happened
- what was filmed
- what production chose to show
- what viewers believe happened

This becomes especially important in the murder mystery.

## Opening Credits and Cast Presentation

Opening-credit placement should be a visible status reward.

Possible progression:

- Guest: absent from main credits
- Friend Of: peripheral promotional presence
- Full-Time: main introduction, symbolic object, tagline
- Center Cast: prestigious placement and stronger visual prominence

Potential cast-presentation elements:

- opening-credit inclusion
- lineup placement
- symbolic object
- tagline
- promo-art prominence
- reunion seating

These can serve as visible seasonal feedback about how production currently views the player.

Being in the center should be desirable but also increase:

- producer expectations
- rivalry
- scrutiny
- risk if the player has a weak season

The game's fictional show should have its own original visual identity, object, branding, and credits convention rather than directly reproducing a real franchise's presentation.

## Taglines

Taglines can be a recurring seasonal feature.

Possible uses:

- production offers options based on the player's current persona or storyline
- the player chooses how they want to frame themselves
- a comeback season can use the tagline to announce a return
- a divorce or scandal can trigger a rebrand
- other cast members may react to the tagline
- the tagline can slightly influence public perception

## Marriage and Family

Marriage and family should remain important even after the player becomes famous.

The reality show can reward behavior that damages the relationship.

Possible marriage dimensions:

### Connection
Do the partners still like, love, and trust one another?

### Pressure
How much strain is fame, filming, conflict, and public attention placing on the relationship?

### Privacy Boundary
How much of the marriage/family is being exposed for content?

### Independence
How much money, identity, and social power does the player now have outside the marriage?

Possible arcs include:

- stable marriage
- spouse embraces the show
- slow deterioration
- public scandal
- affair allegations
- strategic separation
- amicable divorce
- explosive divorce
- player becomes financially independent and leaves
- player chooses to protect the family at the cost of show relevance

Divorce should not automatically be framed as failure.

A player could "succeed" in television while destroying their original life, or leave the show with less fame but a healthier family.

## Multiple Seasons

The game should likely span several fictional seasons rather than compress everything into one continuous story.

A possible progression:

- pre-show / ordinary life
- first guest appearances
- Friend Of season
- full-time cast season
- established-cast season
- murder season

Exact number of seasons remains unresolved.

A season structure creates natural anchors for:

- credits changes
- contract status
- cast additions/removals
- new taglines
- changing alliances
- marriage progression
- business progression
- reunions
- cast trips
- public scandals

## Branching Narrative Structure

The preferred model is not a giant permanently diverging story tree.

Instead:

> Branch meaningfully, reconverge deliberately.

The game can use shared anchor events while preserving consequences from prior choices.

Possible anchors:

- first major party
- first filming event
- cast trip
- reunion
- contract decisions
- major scandal
- murder
- final reveal

Different players may arrive at the same event with different:

- cast status
- alliances
- rivalries
- marriage state
- finances
- public image
- producer relationship
- secrets
- enemies
- past betrayals

A key rule:

> Reconvergence should preserve consequences.

The player can reach the same event as another playthrough without erasing who they became along the way.

## Gossip, Secrets, and Anonymous Accounts

Information should function as currency.

The player can learn things and choose to:

- keep them private
- confront someone
- tell another cast member
- tell production
- leak anonymously
- save the information as leverage
- misdirect suspicion

An anonymous gossip/news account can become a multi-season storyline.

The account may post:

- rumors
- screenshots
- financial claims
- blind items
- relationship information
- behind-the-scenes production details

The player can investigate:

- who knew the leaked information
- when they knew it
- who benefited
- wording patterns
- timing
- whether production knew
- whether multiple people are feeding the same account

The player may be:

- a victim of the account
- an investigator
- an occasional source
- the operator
- framed as the operator
- part of a larger leak network

The culprit does not necessarily need to be the same in every playthrough.

Possibilities include:

- one cast member
- a spouse
- an assistant
- someone outside the cast
- multiple contributors
- one operator with several sources

This storyline can later intersect with the murder mystery.

## Smaller Mysteries Before the Murder

The game should teach investigative thinking gradually through lower-stakes social mysteries.

Potential examples:

### Missing / Stolen Jewelry
An expensive item disappears.

Possible explanations:

- stolen
- misplaced
- borrowed
- planted
- intentionally hidden
- insurance scam

The player may discover the truth but still choose whether to reveal it.

### Possible Affair
There is suspicion that one cast member may be involved with another person's husband or partner.

Possible realities:

- no affair
- inappropriate flirting
- one person initiated
- full affair
- fabricated allegation
- ambiguous evidence

The important mechanic is separating:

1. what the player believes happened
2. what the player can prove
3. what the player chooses to do with the information

### Mystery Injury
Someone appears injured after an event and gives an unclear or conflicting explanation.

The purpose is not to reproduce a real-world reality-TV storyline but to use the structural pattern of uncertainty, conflicting accounts, and delayed revelation.

### Financial Mystery
A cast member's visible lifestyle does not match what the player learns about their actual finances.

### Production Manipulation
The player discovers that conflict that looked organic was partially encouraged or manufactured.

These smaller mysteries should teach:

**collect information -> compare accounts -> decide what you believe -> decide what to reveal**

That becomes the murder-mystery loop later.

## Cast Trip

A cast trip is a natural major event and a strong possible setting for the murder.

It already creates a closed social pressure cooker:

- cast members together for an extended period
- spouses/partners may be present
- production staff nearby
- drinking
- excursions
- room assignments
- arguments
- downtime
- private conversations
- cameras everywhere, but not everywhere

The trip should be playable before the murder occurs.

Possible pre-murder trip interactions:

- choosing roommates
- choosing who to spend time with
- dinners
- excursions
- drinking or staying sober
- snooping
- overhearing conversations
- anonymous messages
- leaked information
- major confrontation
- marriage tension
- missing property
- unexplained absences

The murder should occur late enough that the player already understands the trip's social layout and active tensions.

## Murder Mystery

The murder should grow naturally out of the player's accumulated social world.

The player should arrive at the murder already knowing the suspects, grudges, secrets, marriages, businesses, financial problems, and alliances.

Possible suspects can include:

- current cast members
- former cast members
- spouses / partners
- assistants
- friends
- production staff
- other people established earlier

Existing systems change meaning:

- cast relationships become suspect/witness relationships
- gossip becomes intelligence or misinformation
- financial problems become motive
- marriage problems become motive or alibi
- old leaks become evidence
- production footage becomes incomplete or manipulated evidence
- timelines and event history become investigative tools

A central idea:

> There may be enormous amounts of footage, but footage is not the same thing as truth because everyone was already performing for television.

The mystery can involve both:

- what actually happened
- what version of events was produced for the show

The anonymous-account storyline may become relevant if it:

- posted about the victim
- received a burner tip
- deleted something after the murder
- knew something that had never aired
- was used to plant a false narrative

## Final Choices

Solving the murder should not necessarily be the only ending decision.

Possible final choices may include:

- expose the truth
- protect someone
- protect the player's family
- protect the show
- expose production
- use the truth as leverage
- sell the story
- frame someone
- leave the show
- take control of the cast
- prioritize justice over status
- prioritize status over justice

Exact endings remain unresolved.

## Tone

The target tone is not currently a grim crime thriller.

The preferred blend is closer to:

- camp
- satire
- melodrama
- social simulation
- reality-TV absurdity
- ensemble mystery
- occasional genuine tension

The murder is real, but the surrounding world can remain absurd.

Examples of tonal contrast:

- people worrying about reunion seating during a criminal scandal
- a cast member discussing glam while under investigation
- someone caring about whether a scandal improved their follower count
- production seeing tragedy partly through the lens of what will make television

## Originality / Inspiration Boundary

Reality television can provide inspiration for:

- social structures
- casting hierarchies
- status competition
- cast trips
- gossip
- marriage strain
- off-camera influence
- reunions
- anonymous leaks
- shifting alliances
- money comparison
- public scandal
- production pressure

The game should not lift specific storylines wholesale.

A useful rule:

> Borrow the social dynamics, not the storyline.

Avoid one-to-one versions of real cast members, scandals, dialogue, reveals, or episode plots.

The fictional show should develop its own:

- title
- city / setting
- cast
- branding
- symbolic object
- credits presentation
- taglines
- incidents
- mysteries
- businesses
- marriages
- scandals

The project can use reality-TV examples privately as reference points while developing original expression.

Before a serious commercial release, the project should receive an IP/trademark review of its final title, branding, character designs, opening-credit presentation, and marketing language.

## Current Product Questions

These remain open and should not be silently decided by an implementation agent.

### Core identity
- What is the strongest one-sentence fantasy of the game?
- What should the player feel they are trying to achieve before the murder?

### Early sim
- How deep should work, needs, wardrobe, money, and home management be?
- How quickly should the player move from staying with Nia to their first independent home?
- How many career steps belong before the show becomes meaningfully actionable?
- Which early mechanics are worth keeping because they deepen later?
- How much grind is acceptable before social progression becomes tedious?

### Show structure
- How many fictional seasons should the game span?
- How difficult should promotion/demotion be?
- How much control should the player have over their public persona?
- How much power should producers have?

### Relationships
- How many relationship dimensions are necessary without overwhelming the player?
- How much should marriage/family systems differ by partner?
- How should the dating pool change as the player's social world expands?
- Which early bad dates should be authored comedy versus systemic variation?
- Can the player begin single, married, or either?

### Wealth
- How visible should actual cash/debt be?
- How should perceived wealth differ from actual wealth?
- How much financial risk should be deterministic versus narrative?

### Mysteries
- Which smaller mysteries are fixed versus variable?
- Does the anonymous gossip account have one fixed truth or dynamic culprits?
- Is the murder victim always the same?
- Is the murderer fixed or variable by playthrough?
- How much procedural variation is desirable versus authored mystery design?

### Cast trip
- Is the murder always on the cast trip?
- What destination best supports the fictional show and story?
- Which characters should be present?

### Endings
- Is solving the murder required?
- Can the player knowingly conceal the truth?
- Can the player end the game off the show and still have a successful outcome?
- What constitutes "winning," if anything?
